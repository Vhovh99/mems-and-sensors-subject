# Armenian terminology — the course's single source of truth

> Generated from `hy_terms.py`. Do not edit this file by hand: edit `TERMS` there and
> run `.venv/bin/python i18n/build_glossary.py`. That way the table below and the
> terminology the slides actually use can never disagree.

## Read this first

Between 25 August and 8 September 2026 you corrected the Armenian decks **by hand,
inside the `-HY.pptx` files** (commits `c772767`, `dd2ea8e`, `630bb58`). About 350
paragraphs were rewritten. None of it was in the dictionaries, so the next
`translate_deck.py` run would have thrown all of it away without a word.

That work has been recovered and is now part of the pipeline:

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `hy_terms.py` | **169 canonical terms**, plus 114 enforcement rules applied to every translated string |
| `hy_instructor.py` | **192 of your sentences**, recovered from the decks, loaded last so they override everything |
| `hy_l3.py` / `hy_l4.py` | **the Armenian for Lectures 3 and 4** — 422 and 456 strings |
| `hy_part1…5.py` | the original generated dictionaries, for Lectures 1 and 2 |
| `_term_warnings.txt` | written on every run: the judgement calls left for you |
| `_untranslated.txt` | written on every run: strings with no Armenian (all numerics and register names) |

Three consequences worth knowing:

1. **A term is now fixed in one place.** Changing `resolution` from *լուծաչափ* back to
   *լուծունակություն* is a one-line edit in `hy_terms.py`; it propagates to every slide
   of every lecture on the next run. You will never again correct the same word on
   forty slides.
2. **`translate_deck.py` will no longer overwrite an existing `-HY.pptx`.** It prints
   `· skipping …` instead. Sixteen paragraphs on the L1 figure slides could not be
   recovered mechanically, so a blind rebuild would still lose a little. Pass
   `--overwrite` deliberately, and diff the result, when you are ready.
3. **Lectures 3 and 4 were written against this table**, not against the old
   dictionaries. Their Armenian should need proof-reading, not re-terminologising.
   The 42 strings the run still reports as untranslated are register mnemonics
   (`LPF2_XL_EN`, `ODR_XL3`), physical relations (`k ∝ L`, `C = nεA/d`) and bare
   numerics — all of which are meant to stay Latin.

## Where the terms come from

Three sources, in order of authority:

- **✔ ծրագիր** — fixed by the accredited 2024 programme 1.11.1.17. Changing one of these
  is a conversation with the department, not an editorial choice. It is also where the
  interface vocabulary for Lecture 3 comes from: Theme 2.5 gives *անալոգաթվային
  կերպափոխիչ*, Theme 3.6 gives *հաջորդական պերիֆերային ինտերֆեյս (SPI)*, Theme 3.3 gives
  *ընդհատում*.
- **lab-01 (yours)** — established in `lab-01/prelab-sheet-HY.md`, which you translated.
  It is the authority for bench vocabulary: *սալ*, *ելուստ*, *հում կոդ*, *կազմարկել*,
  *ծրագրային գործիքաշար*.
- **reader** — established in `reader/ch01…-hy.md` and `ch02…-hy.md`, which you
  translated and reviewed end to end. **Where the reader and the old deck dictionaries
  disagreed, the reader won.** It is the reviewed artefact, and it is also what the
  students will be holding.
- **deck** — established by your hand edits to the `-HY` decks.

Terms marked **new** had no Armenian precedent anywhere in the course. They are
Lecture 3 and Lecture 4 vocabulary, and they are the ones most worth your eye.

did: *«20 Հց · ամպլիտուդը դանդաղ աճում է»* but *«ODR = 200 Hz»* and *«µg/√Hz × √Hz = µg»*.

## Register — how the Armenian is written

Your edits were not only lexical. They were consistently more formal than the generated
Armenian, and Lectures 3 and 4 follow that:

- **Declarative, not rhetorical.** *«Ճշմարտությունը բնակվում է պայմաններում»* became
  *«Թվերի իմաստը չափման պայմաններում է»*.
- **Nominal, not verbal.** *«ելքի փոփոխություն մուտքի միավորի հաշվով»* became *«մուտքի մեկ
  միավոր փոփոխությանը համապատասխանող ելքի փոփոխությունը»*.
- **Definitions end in the definite article** `-ը` / `-ն`.
- **No minute markers on student-facing slides.** *«ՀԱՐՑՈՒՄ 1 · ՐՈՊԵ 7»* became
  *«ՔՎԵԱՐԿՈՒԹՅՈՒՆ 1»*; *«ՄԱՍ 1 · ՐՈՊԵ 10–28»* became *«ԲԱԺԻՆ 1»*. Timings live in
  `lecture-plan.md` and the speaker notes. Lectures 3 and 4 carry none on the slides.
- **No instructor-facing text on a student-facing slide** — you replaced
  *«ԴԱՍԱԽՈՍԻՆ. լրացրե՛ք…»* with actual content.
- **Emphasis marks kept where they carry meaning**: *Ե՛վ … և՛*, *Ո՛չ մեկը*, *մի՛ ասեք* —
  but dropped from routine instructions (*Քվեարկե՛ք* → *Քվեարկեք*).
- **Vocabulary of instruction**: *հասկացություն* / *տերմին*, not *բառ*; *ճարտարագիտական*,
  not *ինժեներական*; *հավաստի*, not *վստահելի*; *լաբորատոր աշխատանք*, not *լաբորատորիա*.

## Open questions — these need your decision

`translate_deck.py` writes these to `i18n/_term_warnings.txt` on every run and never
guesses at them.

1. **filter — *զտիչ* or *ֆիլտր*?** Your reader uses **զտիչ** (*հակաալիասինգային զտիչ*).
   The accredited ծրագիր, Theme 2.2, says **Ֆիլտրեր**. Lecture 3 is filter-heavy, so this
   one matters: it is used on eleven L3 slides. The decks currently use *զտիչ* with
   *(ֆիլտր)* glossed on first use, which is defensible either way — but pick one.
2. **Candidate labels — *Սարք Ա/Բ/Գ* or *Սարք A/B/C*?** The L2 deck says *Սարք Ա*, the
   reader says *A բաղադրիչը*, and your own L2 edit says *ՍԱՐՔ C*. All three exist in the
   materials right now. L3 and L4 use Latin letters, following the reader.
3. **cross-axis vs cross-sensitivity.** The materials treat these as one term. They are
   two: *միջառանցքային զգայունություն* is the accelerometer's response to an orthogonal
   axis; *խաչաձև զգայունություն* is a sensor's response to a different measurand
   altogether (a pressure sensor responding to temperature). Lecture 3 needs the
   distinction; the table above adopts it.
4. **mm / µm — Latin or Armenian?** Reader Chapter 1 writes *1–2 մկմ* and *5 մմ*; your L2
   edits write *±20 mm* and *2 m*. Not enforced either way.
5. **microcontroller — *միկրոկառավարիչ* or *միկրոկոնտրոլլեր*?** Your own Lab 1 Armenian
   says **միկրոկառավարիչ**; the accredited ծրագիր (Theme 3.1) and the lecture decks say
   **միկրոկոնտրոլլեր**. Both are in the materials students hold. Lab 2 follows the lab
   precedent, since a student reads the two lab sheets side by side; the decks were left
   alone. One of the two should win.
6. **Speaker notes are still English.** Deliberate — they are yours, not the students'.
   About 8 400 words across four lectures now. Say the word.

## Fitting Armenian into boxes built for English

Armenian runs 10–25 % longer, and slide boxes were sized to the English. The translator
now eases the point size down in two regimes, because the two kinds of string fail
differently:

- **Short strings** (under 34 characters — headings, table row labels, card titles, poll
  options) sit in boxes with no room to reflow, and wrapping one mid-word —
  *Էլեկտրամագնիսակա / ն* — looks broken. These are eased firmly, down to 74 % of the
  original size if necessary.
- **Long strings** are prose in generous boxes and tolerate growth, so they are eased
  gently and keep an effective 14 pt floor.

Where even that was not enough, a dozen labels were shortened by hand in the house style
you established — *Միջառանցք. զգայունություն*, *Աղմուկի սպ. խտություն* — so
*Electromagnetic* is *Էլեկտրամագն.* in the narrow column of the six-principles table, and
the slide footer is trimmed to *MEMS և տվիչներ · Դասախոսություն 4*. Every one of those is
a one-line change in `hy_l3.py` / `hy_l4.py` if you would rather have the full word and
accept the wrap.

## Fonts — do not change casually

Armenian is set in **DejaVu Sans** (DejaVu Sans Mono where the original was monospaced).
This was tested, not guessed:

- **Arial and Courier New have no Armenian glyphs at all.**
- **Noto Sans Armenian was rejected.** It has no Latin digits, so the renderer substitutes
  them one glyph at a time and every number comes out spaced like `0 . 0 6 1`. In a course
  made of numbers that is unusable.
- DejaVu Sans renders Armenian, Latin, digits and `µ √ ± ° ∝` correctly.

The translator switches fonts only on runs that actually contain Armenian, so Latin
numerics keep their metrics. On a machine without DejaVu Sans (most Windows installs) the
Armenian still appears, but substituted; installing DejaVu Sans — it is free — makes the
decks look as intended everywhere.
